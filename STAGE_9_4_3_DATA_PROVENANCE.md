## 1. Experimental Hierarchy & Statistical Unit of Analysis

A common error in reinforcement learning benchmarking is to treat multiple evaluation episodes generated by a single trained neural network policy as independent training replicates (pseudo-replication). To maintain scientific integrity, this study defines a strict two-level experimental hierarchy:

```
Level 1: Independent Experimental Units (N = 3 Seeds per Paradigm)
  ├── Seed 42   (Independent pseudo-random initialization & rollout trajectory during 500k training steps)
  ├── Seed 123  (Independent pseudo-random initialization & rollout trajectory during 500k training steps)
  └── Seed 999  (Independent pseudo-random initialization & rollout trajectory during 500k training steps)

Level 2: Repeated Evaluation Episodes per Model (M = 800 Episodes per Model)
  ├── 6 In-Distribution Conditions (100 episodes each = 600 episodes)
  └── 2 Out-of-Distribution Conditions (100 episodes each = 200 episodes)
```

### Statistical Implications:
1. **Degrees of Freedom ($df = 2$):** Because the study includes $N = 3$ matched seeds across paradigms, all formal hypothesis tests (Paired Student's $t$-tests) have $df = 2$.
2. **Family-Wise Error Rate Control:** With $m = 6$ pairwise comparisons between 4 paradigms, Holm-Bonferroni correction requires $p < 0.00833$ for the first ranked comparison to achieve statistical significance at $\alpha = 0.05$. Because of between-seed variance, **zero pairwise comparisons achieve formal statistical significance**.
3. **Exploratory Empirical Evidence:** The results provide robust directional empirical evidence rather than mathematical proof.

## 3. Four Training Paradigm Definitions

Every benchmark model was trained on Stable-Baselines3 PPO (`MlpPolicy`, $[64, 64]$ tanh) for exactly $500,000$ simulation timesteps using one of four training paradigms:

1. **`A_standard` (Standard PPO Baseline):**
   - Trained on nominal Box2D physics ($g = -10.0\text{ m/s}^2$, zero wind, zero turbulence).
   - Serves as the control baseline. All disturbance conditions represent zero-shot tests.
2. **`B_dr_only` (Domain Randomization Without Scaffolding):**
   - Trained with full domain randomization active from step 0 (randomized gravity $[-13, -7]$, persistent wind up to $25.0$, turbulence up to $2.5$).
   - Demonstrates the failure mode of aggressive uncurriculated domain randomization ($0.3\%$ nominal success).
3. **`C_curriculum_only` (Curriculum Learning Without Randomization):**
   - 4-stage task difficulty progression (Stage 1: Hover $\to$ Stage 2: Gentle Descent $\to$ Stage 3: Offset Launch $\to$ Stage 4: Full Mission).
   - Evaluates developmental scaffolding in nominal physics ($59.3\%$ nominal success).
4. **`D_curriculum_and_dr` (Dual Curriculum + Domain Randomization):**
   - Curriculum stages combined with progressive introduction of domain randomization envelopes.
   - Achieves the highest empirical success rate ($62.0\%$ nominal, $38.8\%$ ID mean, $0.58^\circ$ resting tilt).

## 5. Documented Data Limitations & Scientific Integrity Warnings

1. **The Paradigm B Sample Size Caveat:**
   - Out of 300 evaluation flights in `Standard_Nominal`, Paradigm B completed exactly **one** successful landing ($N=1$, $0.3\%$ success rate).
   - Its resting tilt ($0.06^\circ$) represents a single observation and **must not be compared as a distribution** or cited as superior stability.
2. **Terminal Kinematics Denominator:**
   - On genuine successful landings in Box2D, the lander comes to rest on the pad (`not lander.awake`), meaning true resting velocities are identically zero ($v_x = 0, v_y = 0$).
   - Non-zero terminal velocities reported in benchmark tables reflect the velocity of failed attempts, crashes, and timeouts at the terminal step across all 100 episodes per condition.
3. **No Claim of Proven Statistical Significance:**
   - The Holm-Bonferroni adjusted $p$-value for Paradigm D vs Paradigm A on In-Distribution success is $p_{\text{adj}} = 1.000$ ($t = 1.000, df = 2, p = 0.42265$).
   - While directional superiority is consistent ($38.8\%$ vs $31.2\%$), the sample of $N=3$ training seeds yields overlapping $95\%$ confidence intervals.
